02Before you specify

Five things that decide the job

Module choices
that come back to bite.

General guidance from the supply side of the desk — worth checking before you quote.

  • 01

    Power class and electrical match

    Module wattage has to line up with your inverter's string window and the area you have to fill. Higher wattage per module means fewer modules, less racking, and less labour for the same array size — but only if the string maths works.

  • 02

    Cell technology

    Three of the four power classes are N-type TOPCon; the 540–560 W class is PERC bifacial. TOPCon’s passivating contact layer brings higher efficiency and a better temperature coefficient than older P-type designs, which shows up on hot roofs and in high-irradiance sites.

  • 03

    Bifacial only where it pays

    Bifacial modules collect light on the back face too. That only converts into output when the mounting and the surface below actually reflect — white membrane roofs, ground mounts on light gravel. On dark shingles the gain is largely theoretical.

  • 04

    Physical fit and handling

    Check dimensions and weight against your racking, your lift, and your crew. Larger formats cut labour on commercial roofs and become awkward on steep residential pitches.

  • 05

    Documentation before the quote

    Line up datasheets, warranty terms, and the certifications your AHJ and incentive programme require before you price the job — not after the purchase order is out.
